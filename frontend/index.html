أصول وقواعد مصطلح الحديث (The 5 Golden Criteria of Sahih Hadith)

As defined by Ibn al-Salah (d. 643H) in his famous Muqaddimah and adopted by Al-Nawawi, Ibn Hajar, and the consensus of Hadith masters:
"هو الحديث المسند الذي يتصل إسناده بنقل العدل الضابط عن العدل الضابط إلى منتهاه ولا يكون شاذاً ولا معللاً."

1. اتصال السند (Chain Continuity)

Every narrator in the isnad must have received the Hadith directly from their teacher without any missing link from the compiler up to the Prophet ﷺ.

Chain Breaks: Mu'allaq (beginning missing), Mursal (companion missing), Mu'dal (2+ consecutive missing), Munqati' (1 missing in middle).

2. عدالة الرواة (Moral Probity of Narrators)

Each narrator must be a sane, adult Muslim, free from major sins (Fisq), innovations (Bid'ah), or any reputation of falsehood. All Companions (Sahabah) are upright by divine endorsement.

Jarh Deficiencies: Kadhdhab (fabricator - causes Mawdu'), Matruk (abandoned), Majhul (unknown narrator).

3. ضبط الرواة (Retentive Memory Precision)

Narrators must possess precision, either through flawless oral memory (Dabt al-Sadr) or preserved written manuscripts (Dabt al-Kitab).

Distinction: Tamam al-Dabt (perfect memory -> Sahih) vs. Khafif al-Dabt (sound with slight deficit -> Hasan).

4. السلامة من الشذوذ (Non-Anomaly)

A reliable narrator's report must not contradict reports from narrators who are more trustworthy (Awthaq) or greater in number.

Classifications: Shadhdh (anomalous report) vs. Mahfuz (sound preserved report); Munkar (weak contradicting sound).

5. السلامة من العلة القادحة (Absence of Hidden Defect)

The report must be free from subtle, hidden defects ('Illah Qadihah) that invalidate authenticity despite an apparently sound exterior.

Subtle Flaws: Idraj (inserted text into Matn), Irsal Khafi (concealed drop), and subtle Tadlis.
